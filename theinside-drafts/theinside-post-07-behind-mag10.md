## Concept summary

The "look behind the curtain" post that makes the magazine feel handmade and counters any AI-generated competitor instantly. Nobody writes this kind of post about a kids' magazine because nobody has a story like ours.

Key beats:
- Open with a real number. "Mag10 had 1,847 kid creations submitted. 218 made it into print." Specificity is the whole point.
- The process, walked through:
 - Submissions land via JotForm, often with the kid's parent or teacher's voice attached.
 - Tam (or both of us) reads every single one. From the van. From kitchen tables. From cafes in regional Australia. The point: a real human eyes everything.
 - Curation isn't "best of" - it's "best variety." Every issue needs voices from every state, every age range, every kind of creation. A perfect dragon drawing might miss out if we already have three perfect dragon drawings.
 - The first proofs come back from the printer. We mark them up. Things change. A spread that worked in InDesign doesn't work in real ink.
 - The print run happens in Australia, sustainably (specific printer name if you can name them, otherwise generic).
 - Then Shakira's house becomes packing HQ. Nephews help.
- The honest reflection: every issue, we wish we could include more kids. Every issue, there are creations we love that don't fit. The constraint is what makes the curation meaningful.
- Close with: every Mag is the work of two people, a baby, a van, and 1,800 Aussie kids. AI can't make this. Nobody else is making this.

## FAQ candidates

1. How are creations chosen? (Answer: variety + quality + the gut feeling that this kid's voice belongs in this issue. Not perfectionism. Not curriculum-aligned. Voice-led.)
2. What happens to the creations that don't make it in? (Answer: gallery on the website, possible future issue, possible art prints once that line launches. Nothing gets thrown away.)
3. How do you do this from a van? (Answer: very carefully. Internet connection is sometimes patchy. Tam reviews submissions when we're parked. The actual print files are managed when we're somewhere with proper connectivity.)
