## Concept summary

A surprisingly common parent worry that almost no one writes about properly. Many parents notice their kid (often around age 5-7) draws the same handful of subjects on repeat - hearts and rainbows, dragons, planes, the same one cat, Pokémon characters. They quietly worry: is this a creativity ceiling? A developmental lag? A gendered narrowing?

The actual answer (it's all of those AND it's totally fine AND here's how to gently widen the palette) is reassuring AND useful AND demonstrates Tam's classroom expertise.

Key beats:
- Open with the worry, named clearly. "Your kid has drawn the same dragon 47 times this month and you're starting to wonder."
- The reassurance: drawing the same thing on repeat is how kids master a thing. The dragon at month 6 is a different dragon to the one at month 1, even if they look similar to you. The kid is iterating - refining details, trying new angles, adding context. That's not stagnation, it's craft.
- Howard Gardner's developmental art stages give the academic frame here - kids cycle through schematic representation as they consolidate symbols. The repetition is the consolidation.
- The "but I want them to try something new" parent reframe: don't push, invite. "I love your dragons. I wonder what your dragon would do if it met a unicorn?" The same kid will draw something new, but it'll be on their terms.
- The deeper point: respecting what your kid loves to draw teaches them that what they care about matters. That's a creativity-defining lesson at this age.
- Close with the brand-positive: theINmag publishes lots of "favourite thing" creations because the kids who keep going on the same subject often produce the most striking work in any given issue.

## FAQ candidates

1. Should I worry if it's been more than a year? (Answer: probably not, but it's worth a low-key invitation to draw something new alongside the favourite. If they refuse and seem distressed about it, that's worth a chat with a school counsellor or GP - but it's almost never that.)
2. What if it's specifically gendered (only hearts/rainbows or only weapons/cars)? (Answer: kids absorb cultural cues hard at this age. The fix isn't to take away their favourites, it's to add other options without commentary. Show them other kids' creations across a wider range.)
3. My kid suddenly stopped drawing their favourite thing. Should I be worried? (Answer: probably the post-7 confidence dip - read the post on creativity dropping around age 8, link.)
