## Concept summary

Plain-English answer to the term "rich, open task" - which most parents and even many teachers have heard but don't have a sharp definition for. This is the cornerstone definitional post that the rest of the numeracy cluster links back to.

Key beats:
- A closed task tests whether the kid can do a thing. An open task asks the kid to think about the territory the thing lives in.
- Comparison table: closed vs open task examples side by side, real prompts from primary curriculum, what each tests, what each teaches.
- Peter Sullivan's framing as the canonical Australian definition (multiple solution paths, every kid enters at their own level, no upper limit).
- Why it works: research on multiple exposures across contexts builds durable connected knowledge faster than repeated single-context drill.
- One real example walked through (probably "make 100" since the cluster builds on it, OR a different prompt to avoid repetition - "what's a fair way to share 17 lollies between 4 kids" is a cracker).
- Closing: this is the heart of theINmag's pedagogy. Every issue has open prompts kids can work on at home or in the classroom.
