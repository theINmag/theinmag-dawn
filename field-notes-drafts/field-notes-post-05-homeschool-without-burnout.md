## Concept summary

Homeschool parents are theINmag's most engaged niche audience but the lightest-served on the existing site. The real anxiety this post addresses: planning fatigue, feeling like they're not doing enough, the comparison-to-school worry that creeps in even though they chose the homeschool path deliberately.

The reframe: kid-interest-led planning isn't lazy planning. It's harder, and the kids learn more. Plus a worked example of how Tam would plan a week if she had a 9-year-old who was really into rocks right now.

Key beats:
- Open with the real anxiety. "I'm not doing enough" is the line every homeschool parent has thought, and it's almost always wrong.
- The reframe: school's structured weeks LOOK busier but most of what fills the timetable is logistics (transitions, getting attention, clean-up). A homeschool kid genuinely engaged for two hours has done more learning than a classroom kid in five.
- The interest-led principle: kid is into rocks → that becomes the spine of the week. Reading rocks books, writing about rocks, classifying rocks, calculating their weight in different units, drawing them, going to the geology museum. Every domain gets hit, organically.
- The honest catch: this requires you to genuinely know what your kid is into right now, which means you've been paying attention. Most parents underestimate this skill in themselves.
- Close with: theINmag is built for exactly this kind of family, because every issue has prompts kids can take in any direction.

## FAQ candidates

1. What if my kid's interests are too narrow / too weird / not "educational"? (Answer: there's no such thing as a narrow interest - kids will pull learning out of anything they love. Pokemon is a literacy curriculum. Minecraft is a maths curriculum.)
2. How do I know they're actually learning? (Answer: ask them to explain something they care about. Recall is the lowest form of learning - explanation is high-order. Most homeschool kids run circles around classroom kids on this measure.)
3. What about the bits they don't want to learn? (Answer: most of them are still findable through interests, and the ones that aren't can be done in 15-minute focused sessions. You don't need a 90-minute "maths block" if the maths is genuine.)
